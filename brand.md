# VYBE — Brand Foundation

**Status:** Brand foundation v1.0  
**Brand:** VYBE  
**Category:** Personalized live sports experience  
**Primary launch market:** India  
**Phase-one sports:** Cricket  
**Planned expansion:** Football, Tennis, and additional live sports  

---

## 1. Brand Core

### Brand Name
# VYBE

Pronounced like **“vibe.”**

Always write the brand as **VYBE** in uppercase in master-brand contexts.

Do not append “AI” to the master brand. AI is the technology underneath the experience; **VYBE is the experience users choose and remember.**

### Master Tagline
# Same game. Your vibe.

This is the primary expression of the brand and should remain stable across products, markets, and sports.

### Primary Product CTA
# Choose your VYBE.

This is the core interaction language. It should appear whenever a user is choosing a language, commentator persona, tone, or experience.

### Core Launch Context
> **India vs Australia is live.**  
> **Choose your VYBE.**

This is the canonical example of how the brand should introduce the product without explaining the underlying technology.

---

## 2. What VYBE Is

VYBE lets fans experience live sports in the **language, voice, personality, and energy they choose**.

The same live match can become different experiences for different people:

- Hindi + Gen Z
- Gujarati + Fan
- Tamil + Pro
- Marathi + Hype
- English + Analyst

The underlying technology is AI-powered live translation and voice generation with low latency. The customer-facing story is not “AI dubbing.”

The customer-facing story is:

> **The same game, experienced your way.**

### One-line Brand Definition
> **VYBE is the personalized live-sports experience that lets fans choose how they experience the game.**

### Category Language
Preferred:
- The personalized live-sports experience
- Personalized live sports
- Watch it your way / sports, your way
- A new way to experience live sports

Avoid leading with:
- Commentary as the product noun (see the rule below)
- AI dubbing platform
- Multilingual localization platform
- Neural voice synthesis
- AI commentary infrastructure

**The experience rule (added 2026-08-18):** commentary is the mechanism,
not the product. Nobody buys commentary; fans buy the game in their
language, their energy, their vibe. Consumer surfaces sell the
EXPERIENCE — "commentary" and "commentator" may appear in technical
docs and deep product copy, never as the headline noun of a consumer
message. When a consumer line needs the concept, say "your VYBE" or
"the match, your way".

Technical language can explain the product deeper in the funnel. It should not define the brand.

## 3. Brand Promise

### Functional Promise
Experience the live game in the language and energy that feel natural to you.

### Emotional Promise
You do not have to experience the game the same way as everyone else.

### Brand Belief
> **There is only one game. There is no single way to experience it.**

Language is not merely a translation setting. It is identity, familiarity, humor, culture, and emotion.

VYBE turns those differences into part of the product.

## 4. Brand Positioning

### The Old Model
One live broadcast. One commentary experience.

### The VYBE Model
One live broadcast. Multiple personalized experiences.

### The Product Equation
> **Language + Personality + Live Sport = Your VYBE**

VYBE is not replacing the game. It is creating an additional layer of choice around the game.

## 5. Brand Personality

VYBE should feel:

### Confident
We know the product is different. We do not over-explain it.

### Energetic
The brand belongs in moments of excitement, tension, celebration, and reaction.

### Culturally Fluent
VYBE understands how fans actually speak, joke, react, celebrate, and argue about sport.

### Playful
There is room for humor, personality, internet culture, and unexpected commentary experiences.

### Smart
The technology is sophisticated, but the brand never sounds like an engineering demo.

### Inclusive
There is no single “correct” way to enjoy a match.

### Personality Formula
**Confident × Energetic × Culturally Fluent × Playful × Smart**

VYBE should feel modern without trying desperately to “sound Gen Z.”

---

## 6. Tone of Voice

### VYBE sounds like
- A confident friend who knows sport
- A sharp digital entertainment brand
- Someone culturally aware and quick with a reaction
- A brand that is comfortable being playful

### VYBE does not sound like
- A corporate broadcast network
- A generic AI startup
- A telecommunications company
- A brand trying to imitate Gen-Z slang
- A technical documentation page

### Copy Principles

**Short beats clever.**  
Use compact statements that land quickly.

**Conversational beats corporate.**  
Write like people speak, not like product brochures.

**Energy beats explanation.**  
Show what the experience feels like before explaining how it works.

**Culture beats generic “localization.”**  
Languages and personas should feel like real identities, not checkboxes.

**Use internet language carefully.**  
Words like “vibe,” “hype,” “lock in,” or “coded” can appear in campaign copy and persona naming, but they should never make the whole brand feel dated.

### The Hype

## 12. Messaging We Should Avoid

Avoid phrases that make VYBE sound like a technical utility:

> “Translate live sports commentary into regional languages.”

> “AI-powered multilingual audio localization.”

> “Choose from multiple synthesized commentary voices.”

> “Real-time neural speech generation.”

These may appear in technical documentation or detailed product pages, but they should not be the primary consumer message.

Avoid forced slang such as:

> “No cap, this match is bussin fr fr.”

The brand should be **Gen-Z fluent, not Gen-Z cosplay.**

# 13. Visual Brand Direction

The logo will be developed separately. Until then, all visual decisions should reinforce:

### Voice
Audio waves, pulses, sonic movement.

### Energy
Motion, dynamic typography, quick transitions.

### Choice
Switches, states, layers, selectable experiences.

### Culture
Language-specific expressions and personality without falling into cliché regional stereotypes.

### Live
Visual systems should feel reactive and in-motion rather than static.

### Avoid
Avoid making cricket balls, footballs, microphones, trophies, or broadcast antennas the primary visual language. These are too literal and make VYBE look like a conventional sports media property.

# 14. Brand Colors

The palette should feel **young, digital, energetic, premium, and slightly rebellious** while remaining flexible enough to sit beside live sports footage.

## Primary Palette

### VYBE Black
**#0A0A0F**

Use for:
- Primary backgrounds
- App chrome
- Premium/dark experiences
- Major campaign surfaces

This is the brand's visual anchor.

### VYBE Violet
**#7357FF**

Use for:
- Primary brand color
- Buttons and active states
- Brand graphics
- Headlines/accent moments
- Digital UI highlights

This is the recognizable VYBE identity color.

### VYBE Lime
**#D8FF3E**

Use for:
- High-energy accents
- CTAs in dark contexts
- Live indicators
- Selected states
- Key words and visual highlights

Lime should be used as a **signal**, not as a background everywhere.

### VYBE White
**#F7F7F2**

Use for:
- Primary text on dark backgrounds
- Clean layouts
- Product surfaces
- Editorial communication

The slight warmth keeps the palette from feeling sterile.

---

## Secondary Neutrals

### VYBE Slate
**#17171F**

Cards, panels, elevated dark surfaces.

### VYBE Grey
**#8E8E9A**

### VYBE Mist
**#E8E8E3**

## Signature Gradient

Use sparingly for hero visuals, campaign art, motion, and special moments:

**VYBE Violet → VYBE Lime**

`#7357FF → #D8FF3E`

The gradient should feel like **energy moving through the brand**, not like a decorative rainbow.

---

## 15. Color Usage Rules

Recommended approximate distribution for a major VYBE surface:

**60% VYBE Black / dark neutrals**  
**25% VYBE White / light neutrals**  
**10% VYBE Violet**  
**5% VYBE Lime**

The key principle:

> **Purple identifies the brand. Lime creates the moment.**

Do not use lime everywhere. Its power comes from appearing at moments of action, selection, excitement, and “live now.”

### Accessibility
Use **VYBE Lime primarily against VYBE Black or other very dark backgrounds**. For small text, do not place lime on white. Use strong contrast combinations for product UI and informational content.

# 20. Brand Guardrails

### Always
- Put the fan at the center.
- Lead with experience, not technology.
- Make choice visible.
- Treat language as identity and culture, not a checkbox.
- Let the product feel alive.
- Use humor and internet culture selectively.
- Keep the master brand language simple enough to travel globally.

### Never
- Make VYBE sound like a voice-synthesis company.
- Overuse technical AI terminology.
- Force regional stereotypes.
- Use slang just to appear young.
- Make every execution neon or visually noisy.
- Lock the product identity to cricket.
- Lock the brand to India-only language conventions.

---

# 21. Brand North Star

If a future feature does not support the idea of **personalized live sports experience**, it should be questioned.

If a campaign cannot be summarized as **“Same game. Your vibe.”**, the campaign needs a clearer connection to the brand.

If a user can naturally say **“I chose my VYBE”**, the product language is working.

---

# 22. The VYBE Test

Before approving any future name, feature, campaign, screen, or piece of copy, ask:

1. **Does this feel like VYBE, not generic AI?**
2. **Does it make the fan feel in control?**
3. **Does it communicate energy or personality?**
4. **Could it eventually work across sports and languages?**
5. **Would someone actually say it out loud?**

If the answer is yes to all five, it is likely on-brand.
